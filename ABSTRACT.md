# Estimated Roster Resources and College Football Outcomes Beyond the Closing Market

## Introduction

College football roster compensation has become a prominent explanation for competitive success. We examined whether estimated resource disparities provide information about game outcomes beyond the closing sportsbook market.

## Methods

We reconstructed 347 games from the 2025 season involving teams in a frozen resource table covering 68 programs. Published low and high estimates of annual football roster compensation included institutional revenue sharing and third-party compensation. These retrospective estimates were not audited payroll figures. Closing spreads and totals came from a predefined historical archive, supplemented by nine labeled playoff games. One game with a missing score remained excluded from outcome calculations. We documented a score correction and a resource-midpoint join correction in a new dataset version and reran all primary models. Resource gap was the difference between teams’ estimated compensation midpoints divided by the lower midpoint. Linear models of scoring margin and logistic models of win probability controlled for the signed closing spread. Dyadic-robust uncertainty estimates accounted for games sharing a team; conventional robust estimates provided additional checks. Secondary analyses used fixed resource tiers and a 500-draw stress test across published compensation ranges. The historical reconstruction and secondary analyses were not preregistered.

## Results

Higher-resource teams won 201 of 308 resolved comparisons (65.3%). After adjustment for the closing spread, the scoring-margin coefficient was −1.295 points per unit of fractional resource gap (dyadic SE 3.629; 95% CI −8.408 to 5.818; p=.721). The win-probability model’s logit coefficient was −.226 (SE .507; 95% CI −1.219 to .767; p=.655). Conventional robust p-values were .726 and .704, respectively. Resource gap did not detectably predict absolute spread error or signed or absolute total error in the specified linear models. The association between the three fixed resource tiers and Over/Under outcomes was weak (p=.325), substantially smaller than the earlier development-sample pattern. Stress tests that sampled uniformly within published ranges yielded a median higher-resource win rate of 61.6%, illustrating uncertainty in which teams held a resource advantage.

## Conclusion

Higher estimated roster resources were associated descriptively with winning. The specified models detected no additional resource-gap information beyond the closing market. Wide confidence intervals, retrospective measurement, repeated teams and a single season limit generalization. These findings do not establish causation or a validated betting rule. Further research should cover more seasons, improve compensation measurement, and examine the financial burden of existing contracts and informal commitments involving coaches, players and boosters. It should also test how quickly sportsbook markets respond to unusually large resource changes and other unexpected developments.
